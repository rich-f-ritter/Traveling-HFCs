# 01 — Legal Framework: the statutes and kill dates that define the target set

All verified against enrolled bill text and current codification (see `reference/` for the
citation trail). Statute text tip: statutes.capitol.texas.gov now serves a JS app shell to
non-browser clients — for machine-readable text use TDHCA's certified PDF
(tdhca.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pmcdocs/Ch394-LGC.pdf), FindLaw, or texas.public.law.

## The two vehicles

| | HFC | PFC |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | Local Gov't Code **Ch. 394** (Housing Finance Corporations Act) | Local Gov't Code **Ch. 303** (Public Facility Corporations Act) |
| Sponsor | city or county | cities, counties, housing authorities, school districts, others |
| Exemption | **§394.905** — HFC property "exempt, as public property used for public purposes, from … all other taxes" → 100% ad valorem | §303.042 |
| Traveling loophole closed | **HB 21** (89R, eff. 5/28/2025) | **HB 2071** (88R, 2023) — §303.021(d): multifamily must sit inside sponsor boundaries (housing-authority sponsors: "area of operation") |
| Audit regime | §394.9027 → TDHCA | §303.0426 → TDHCA |

Both produce the identical structure: developer conveys fee to the public corporation →
property leaves the tax roll entirely → 99-year ground lease back to the developer entity →
sponsor collects fees (closing fee, % -of-savings annual fee, per-unit compliance fee).
"Traveling" = sponsor's home jurisdiction ≠ property's county. The market calls all of it
"PFC deals," which is why entity names, not deal nicknames, are the reliable search keys.

## HB 21's kill mechanics (the definition of "going away")

1. **Geography (new §394.031(c)–(d)):** HFC may own residential-development property only
   inside its sponsor's boundaries, unless EVERY affected host (each municipality; each
   county for unincorporated areas; any local HFC they sponsor) adopts an approving
   resolution. Applies to acquisitions on/after 5/28/2025.
2. **§13(i) — the census-defining cutoff:** a development owned by an HFC on **9/1/2025**
   and located outside its authorized area is ineligible for the §394.905 exemption
   **after 1/1/2027** unless the §394.031(d) resolutions are obtained before that date.
   → *The target list = HFC-owned multifamily, out-of-jurisdiction as of 9/1/2025, without
   host resolutions.* Track resolution adoptions (city council agendas) as removals.
3. **§13(e) — early death on capital events:** pre-5/28/2025 deals keep the exemption only
   until the earlier of the 10th tax year or **the end of the first tax year after a
   refinancing, title conveyance, or majority beneficial-interest transfer**. → Every
   listed property that trades or refis converts to full tax on a knowable date.
4. **New ongoing conditions (§394.905(b), §394.9026):** board resolution + underwriting
   assessment projecting rent reductions ≥50% of foregone taxes; set-asides (10% at ≤60% AMI
   + 40% at ≤80%, or 10% at ≤50% + 40% at ≤100%); rent caps at 30% of the band; annual
   pass-through test (with a pay-the-shortfall cure, §394.9026(c)(2)(B)). Legacy flat
   "50% at <80% AMI" covenants FAIL this formula — even rescued deals need re-papering.
5. **§394.9027 audits:** annual independent audit filed with TDHCA (first due **6/1/2026**);
   TDHCA publishes a findings summary within 60 days (§394.9027(c)); the audit itself is
   TPIA-disclosable with tenant-identifying redactions (§394.9027(k)). **Failure to file
   forfeits the exemption for that tax year** — an independent, earlier death than 2027.
6. **Transparency (§394.0045):** the Public Information Act applies to **all HFC records**
   (no grandfathering); Open Meetings Act applies to proceedings. This is the project's
   master key — every ground lease, MOU, fee schedule, and board minute is compellable.

## HB 2071's PFC side (handle as a separate tier)

Verified core: §303.021(d) geographic restriction (2023) + a TDHCA audit regime
(§303.0426); pre-existing deals **grandfathered**. ⚠️ Two circulating "facts" were REFUTED
in our verification and must not be repeated without checking the enrolled text: the
specific tiered set-aside percentages attributed to HB 2071, and a "$40/unit audit fee
applied retroactively to all pre-existing PFC deals." Re-verify any PFC-side specifics
against HB 2071's enrolled text before publishing.

## The litigation layer (moves fast; monitor)

- **Pleasanton HFC v. City of Lake Worth / City of Arlington** — 15th Court of Appeals
  Nos. **15-25-00110-CV / 15-25-00111-CV**: temporary injunctions against the traveling
  model itself, on appeal. The pleadings describe the structure in detail (admissions).
- **PHFC appraisal-district case** — Fort Worth COA **02-25-00474-CV**.
- **AG opinion request RQ-0566-KP** (10/2/2024, House Urban Affairs chair/vice-chair):
  argues §394.903(a) *already* barred out-of-boundary development pre-HB 21 — if the AG
  agrees, exemptions on traveling deals may be attackable retroactively (back-assessment
  under Tax Code §11.43(i) reaches the **five preceding years**). Watch for the opinion.
- **HB 21 constitutional challenge** (Texas Workforce Housing Coalition + Cameron County,
  filed ~Sept 2025): aimed at the retroactive tax provisions, not §394.0045; no injunction
  found as of 7/11/2026. If it succeeds, the 2027 cliff moves — the census should record
  its status at each publication.

## Tax mechanics that matter for valuing each unwind

- Lien attaches **Jan 1** (Tax Code §32.01); bills ~Oct 1 (§31.01); delinquent Feb 1
  (§31.02). Exemption death on 1/1/2027 = first full-tax bill Oct 2027, cash due Jan 2028.
- Erroneously allowed exemptions: chief appraiser back-assesses up to **five preceding
  years** — Tax Code **§11.43(i)** (§25.21 alone is now 3-yr real / 2-yr personal; cite
  §11.43(i) for the exemption case).
- Texas is a **non-disclosure state**: sale prices aren't reported to CADs; assessments on
  these properties often exceed distressed trade prices → the post-unwind tax bill is a
  protest-to-basis question, worth modeling as a range.
