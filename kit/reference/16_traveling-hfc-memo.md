## Bottom line

**The Sarah at Lake Houston is an HFC deal — a Chapter 394 *housing finance corporation* structure — not a PFC deal.** Our own deal file's habit of calling it "the PFC structure" (including `01_deal-overview.md` describing Pleasanton HFC as a "public facility corporation") is a market colloquialism that is legally wrong, and the distinction is load-bearing: PFCs (Chapter 303) had their traveling loophole closed by **HB 2071 in 2023**; HFCs kept traveling for two more years until **HB 21 (effective May 28, 2025)** — and it is HB 21, not HB 2071, whose January 1, 2027 exemption-termination provision drives this deal's unwind. A buyer should assume the exemption **does not survive an acquisition in any scenario**: the out-of-jurisdiction cutoff catches it on 1/1/2027, the grandfather dies early on a sale or refi anyway, and the current Regulatory Agreement fails HB 21's new set-aside formula even if everything else were cured.

## 1. What an HFC is, and what makes one "traveling"

A **housing finance corporation** is a nonprofit public corporation that a Texas city or county creates under the [Texas Housing Finance Corporations Act, Local Government Code Chapter 394](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=LG&Value=394), historically to issue tax-exempt bonds for affordable housing. The feature that built an industry is **Sec. 394.905**: an HFC's property is *"exempt, as public property used for public purposes, from license fees, recording fees, and all other taxes imposed by this state or any political subdivision"* — a **100% ad valorem exemption**, with no statutory affordability formula attached (pre-HB 21) beyond what the HFC chose to impose by contract.

The **"traveling"** variant works like this (verified 3-0, documented in litigation against this deal's own counterparty):

1. A small city or county's HFC — here, **Pleasanton** (Atascosa County, pop. ~11,000) — offers its governmental status to developers statewide.
2. A private owner **conveys fee title** to the HFC; the property comes **off the tax rolls entirely** under Sec. 394.905.
3. The HFC **ground-leases the property back** to the private operator (often the same developer) for 99 years, collecting fees and profit-sharing — here a $300K closing fee, a **Savings Fee of 10% of the tax savings**, and compliance fees.
4. The host jurisdiction — whose schools, city, MUD, and county lose the revenue — **had no say**. Pre-HB 21, nothing in Chapter 394 confined an HFC's property ownership to its sponsor's boundaries.

The Senate sponsor of HB 21 (Sen. Bettencourt) framed the target explicitly: developers "partnering with out-of-jurisdiction traveling HFCs" to receive "full property tax exemptions for multifamily housing, with no rent reductions" ([Senate press release, 5/28/2025](https://senate.texas.gov/press.php?id=7-20250528b)). Pleasanton HFC itself is the defendant-appellant in the leading litigation: a Tarrant County court enjoined its Lake Worth acquisition (~302 miles from home), now on appeal in the new Texas 15th Court of Appeals (Nos. 15-25-00110-CV, -00111-CV; the [appellate record](https://app.midpage.ai/document/pleasanton-housing-finance-corporation-a-11090152) quotes the mechanics above; note the harm figures — ~$1.22M/yr across taxing units — come from the city's pleadings, not judicial findings).

## 2. HFC vs. PFC — the difference, precisely

| | **HFC** | **PFC** |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | Local Gov't Code **Chapter 394** (Housing Finance Corporations Act) | Local Gov't Code **Chapter 303** (Public Facility Corporations Act) |
| Sponsor | A city or county | Broader: cities, counties, housing authorities, school districts, other sponsors |
| Exemption | Sec. **394.905** ("public property used for public purposes") | Chapter 303's exemption provisions (Sec. 303.042) |
| Traveling loophole closed by | **HB 21 (89th Leg., 2025)** — new Sec. 394.031(c)–(d) | **HB 2071 (88th Leg., 2023)** — new Sec. 303.021(d): multifamily must be inside the sponsor's boundaries (or a housing authority's "area of operation") |
| Post-reform compliance regime | Set-asides + rent-reduction pass-through + rent caps (Sec. 394.9026); annual independent audit filed with TDHCA (Sec. 394.9027) | Parallel TDHCA audit regime (Sec. 303.0426); HB 2071's grandfathering preserved pre-2023 PFC deals |
| Sources | [HB 21 enrolled text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB00021F.htm) | [HB 2071 enrolled text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB02071F.htm) |

**Why the market conflates them:** both produce the identical economic object — governmental fee title + 99-year ground lease + 100% exemption + fees to the sponsor — so brokers and sellers call the whole category "PFC deals." HB 2071's 2023 crackdown on PFCs is precisely *why* the deal volume migrated to HFCs in 2023–2025: Chapter 394 was the chapter the legislature hadn't fixed yet. The Sarah (PFC-style deal closed **February 2025** through an **HFC**) is a specimen of that migration — it could not have been done as a PFC at all, because Sec. 303.021(d) had already required PFC multifamily to sit inside the sponsor's boundaries since 2023.

*(Two granular HB 2071 claims — the PFC set-aside tier percentages and a $40/unit audit fee with retroactive application — were refuted in verification and are deliberately omitted; re-verify against the enrolled HB 2071 text before relying on PFC-side specifics.)*

## 4. HB 21 — what changed, section by section

HB 21 (companion SB 867) passed 115–13 / 30–1 and took **effect immediately on May 28, 2025** ([enrolled text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB00021F.htm); [Chapman](https://www.chapman.com/publication-texas-housing-finance-corporations-act-amended-by-hb-21-sb-867-effective-may-28-2025), [Butler Snow](https://www.butlersnow.com/news-and-events/major-reforms-to-chapter-394-hb-21-brings-sweeping-changes-to-housing-finance-corporations-in-texas), [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/texas-enacts-new-legislation-that-will-reshape-affordable) alerts). All verified 3-0 against the bill text:

**a. Geographic confinement — Sec. 394.031(c)–(d).** An HFC may own real property for residential development, develop, or bond-finance **only within its sponsor's boundaries**, unless *every* affected host adopts an approving resolution: each municipality containing any part of the area, each county (for unincorporated areas), **and any HFC sponsored by those local governments**. Applies to property acquired on or after 5/28/2025.

**b. New conditions on the exemption itself — Sec. 394.905(b) + Sec. 394.9026.** For multifamily, the exemption now requires: location within the authorized area (or resolutions); an HFC board resolution supported by an underwriting assessment (≤180 days old) projecting **rent reductions ≥50% of the foregone taxes**; a one-time filing with TDHCA and each chief appraiser; and *annually*, set-asides of **10% of units at ≤60% AMI + 40% at ≤80% AMI** (or 10% at ≤50% + 40% at ≤100%), rents capped at 30% of the band, and the 50% rent-reduction pass-through (with an alternative cure of paying taxing units the pro-rata shortfall — Sec. 394.9026(c)(2)(B)). LIHTC deals are carved out.

**c. Annual audit regime — Sec. 394.9027.** Independent compliance audit at the HFC user's expense, filed with TDHCA annually (TDHCA's program set the **first reports due June 1, 2026**); TDHCA publishes findings within 60 days; 180-day cure; unresolved noncompliance **or failure to file** forfeits the exemption for that tax year ([TDHCA HFC Compliance Monitoring](https://www.tdhca.texas.gov/housing-finance-corporation-compliance-monitoring)).

**d. The two transition rules that decide this deal (uncodified Section 13):**

- **Section 13(i) — the 1/1/2027 guillotine.** A development owned by an HFC on **September 1, 2025** and located outside its authorized area is **not eligible for the Sec. 394.905 exemption after January 1, 2027**, unless the Sec. 394.031(d) resolutions are obtained **before that date**. The Sarah was owned by PHFC out-of-jurisdiction on 9/1/2025 → squarely captured.
- **Section 13(e) — grandfathering that dies on capital events.** Pre-5/28/2025 acquisitions keep the exemption only if they meet the tenant-protection items by 1/1/2026 and the core affordability requirements (set-asides, rent-reduction test, rent caps) by the **earlier of** the 10th tax year (effectively 12/31/2035) **or the end of the first tax year after a refinancing, a conveyance of title, or a transfer of a majority of beneficial ownership interests**. A purchase or refi of The Sarah therefore collapses the runway on its own, independent of 13(i).

## 5. Application to The Sarah through the 2027 unwind

1. **Base case: the exemption ends 1/1/2027.** Rescue requires approving resolutions from the City of Humble (plus Harris County/any local HFC as applicable) before 12/31/2026 — under six months from today, from host jurisdictions the statute was written to protect, in favor of an HFC currently enjoined in parallel litigation. Assign this near-zero probability.
2. **A buyer cannot ride any grandfather.** The acquisition itself (title conveyance or majority-interest transfer; a refi counts too) triggers Section 13(e) acceleration. Underwrite **full taxes from the first tax year after closing** — consistent with the TMG model's $54M-at-full-taxes framing and our cash-flow model's 2027 projection (~$1.53M/yr tax; NOI ~$755K/qtr).
3. **Even the rescue scenario fails on the covenant.** The Regulatory Agreement's flat 50%-at-<80%-AMI does not satisfy Sec. 394.9026(c)'s deep-affordability band (no 10% at ≤60%/≤50% AMI), and the deal has no 50% rent-reduction pass-through mechanism — the T12 shows the tax savings largely retained (the Exhibit B forensics found a real but shallow −8.5% median discount). Retaining the exemption would require re-cutting the covenant *and* passing annual TDHCA audits.
4. **The unwind lever is clean and cheap.** MOU Section H: reconveyance to the owner for **$10** on written request; the Regulatory Agreement terminates when the HFC ceases to own the land (sole remedy: specific performance; no clawback). Frictions to budget: the HFC's **30-day ROFR** on any sale, lender consent (the A&R DoT voids ground-lease surrender without beneficiary consent, plus the fee-acquisition spring), and the 2/21/2025 Operating Agreement's managing-member mechanics.
5. **Counterparty risk is live.** The fee owner of this asset is litigating the legality of its business model in two appeals (Lake Worth, Arlington). An adverse ruling, settlement, or receivership before closing could complicate PHFC's performance under the ground lease, ROFR, and reconveyance obligations — a diligence item, not a valuation item.

**Net for underwriting:** the only defensible case is **all 350 units market-rate at full taxation post-closing** — which is exactly the 2027 hand-off panel in Exhibit C: tax $153K → ~$1.53M/yr, offset by the +$549K/yr affordable-reversion recapture at stabilization, against a market-cohort rent anchor of ~$1,470–1,500 that held through the submarket's slide.
