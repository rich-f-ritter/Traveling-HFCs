# Adversarial Verification — "North Texas CADs Have Already Stripped the HFC Exemptions"

*Prepared 2026-07-11 as an independent skeptical check on the Phase-3 wave-1 headline. All
evidence below was re-pulled directly from the appraisal districts' own free public portals
(tad.org, dallascad.org, bexar.trueautomation.com) and free press, on 2026-07-11. Method:
re-fetched 13 individual CAD account pages from scratch and compared each against the census
flag; searched free news coverage for the enforcement mechanism.*

---

## VERDICT: **CONFIRMED** (core fact) — with a **material mechanism correction**

**The load-bearing claim survives.** A large majority of the traveling Chapter-394 HFC parcels
are, right now, carried at **full taxable value with no §11.11 exemption** on the CADs' own
current (2026) rolls — and this is a real, deliberate appraisal-district action taken **years
ahead of the 1/1/2027 statutory cliff**, not a data artifact. I re-verified **13 of 13** sampled
parcels directly against the live portals; every one matched the census flag exactly (owner,
deed date, exemption code, taxable value). The Dallas count is independently corroborated by
press: DCAD publicly **denied ~120 HFC exemption requests**, adding **~$3 billion** back to the
roll and **~$50 million/yr** in Dallas tax bills — a figure that lines up with the census's own
independently-derived **119 of 132**.

**But two framings in the wave-1 write-ups are wrong or unsourced and must be corrected:**

1. **"TAD quietly stopped granting the exemption ≈ February 2025" is an unsourced inference, and
   it does not generalize.** The Tarrant deed-date correlation is real (I verified it at the
   boundary), but **no public source dates or attributes a deliberate TAD policy change to Feb
   2025**, and TAD's own chief appraiser is on record *defending* TAD's role in granting
   exemptions while the **County** pushes to remove them. Dallas shows **no Feb-2025 cutoff at
   all** — DCAD carries a **December-2024** Pecos acquisition (Innova) as fully taxable, while
   Tarrant still grants the exemption on a near-identical December-2024 Pecos deed. The "Feb-2025
   cutoff" is a Tarrant-only pattern, not a "North Texas" rule.

2. **"Stripped" conflates two distinct, separately-sourced mechanisms.** The dominant driver of
   the big "no-exemption" counts is the **chief appraiser DENYING recent (TY2025/26) exemption
   applications** on a new "these HFCs were never authorized to own out-of-jurisdiction property"
   theory (Dallas: ~120 denials). That is legally *denial of a new/recent claim*, not removal of a
   posted exemption. A **separate** mechanism — **taxing-unit ARB petitions and court suits to
   claw back still-active older exemptions** (Tarrant County's 28-site / $974M ARB petition;
   Arlington/Fort Worth TROs) — targets the parcels that are *still exempt*. Both run ahead of
   the cliff; both are now being **litigated by developers as unconstitutionally retroactive**, so
   the taxable status of these parcels is contingent, not settled.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the core fact (roll data + independent DCAD-denial reporting triangulate).
**MEDIUM** on the exact per-bucket split (a "proposed"/uncertified 2026 roll cannot cleanly
separate *formally-denied* from *pending/never-filed* for the newest acquisitions; the "~120"
figure originates with an interested party, the Dallas HFC board, though carried by multiple
outlets).

---

## Dominant mechanism

**Chief-appraiser denial of the §11.11 / §394.905 public-property exemption on recent HFC
acquisitions, applied early (for tax years 2025 and 2026) across multiple counties** — on the
districts' reversed legal position that traveling HFCs were never authorized to hold
out-of-jurisdiction property. Layered on top: **local taxing units (counties/cities) challenging
the older, still-active exemptions** via ARB petition and litigation (retroactive up to 5 years),
and **developers counter-suing** to void HB 21 and the early denials as retroactive.

Sources: appraisal districts "acting sooner, denying exemptions for 2025 and 2026 in several
counties" and DCAD's ~120 denials / ~$3B / ~$50M-per-year, per The Center Square / Dallas
Express / El Paso Inc. (syndicated), 2025; Tarrant County ARB petition (28 sites / $974M, filed
5/28/2025) per Tarrant County & KERA/Fort Worth Report; Arlington/Fort Worth TRO and the
TWHC/Post WB constitutional suit vs. Bexar AD (filed 9/9/2025, Cameron County District Court) per
The Texan / News4SanAntonio / Multifamily Dive.

**Key correction:** most "N" parcels belong in **A as *denials of a recent claim*, not

HFC bought it (2024–2025), and the CAD refused or has not posted the new exemption. Only bucket **B**
parcels ever had a posted exemption that an authority is now removing.

## Task-4 verdict — "TAD stopped granting the exemption ≈ Feb 2025"

**REFUTED as a sourced claim / UNSUPPORTED as stated; the underlying data pattern is real.**
The census's Tarrant deed-date correlation genuinely exists and I confirmed it at the boundary
(3/18/2025 deed still exempt; 2/6/2025 deed already taxable — a ~6-week fuzzy transition, not a
clean date). **But nothing in the public record dates a deliberate TAD administrative change to
February 2025.** The documented Tarrant enforcement action is the opposite posture: **Tarrant
County** filing ARB petitions (5/28/2025) to strip exemptions **TAD had granted**, with TAD's
chief appraiser maintaining "TAD should be the initial entity determining the exemptions." The
early-denial phenomenon is real and multi-county (best-sourced in **Dallas**, via DCAD's ~120
denials), but the specific "TAD, February 2025" attribution is an inference from the roll, not a
fact from a source, and should be down-graded to "TAD appears to have tightened on new HFC
applications during 2025" pending a TPIA request for TAD's denial letters / ARB determinations.
