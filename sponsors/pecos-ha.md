# Pecos "Housing Authority" — Phase 1 Sponsor Profile

**Slug:** pecos-ha · **Seed count:** 126 properties (largest single sponsor in the seed) ·
**Seed classification:** Housing Authority (Ch. 392), Tier 3 · **As of:** 2026-07-11

> ## HEADLINE FINDING — THE SEED IS MISLABELED. RECLASSIFY Tier 3 → Tier 1.
>
> The Yardi Matrix seed attributes these 126 traveling multifamily deals to the
> **Pecos Housing Authority** (Ch. 392, Tier 3 — a regime HB 21/HB 2071 do *not* touch).
> **The weight of primary evidence (appraisal-district owner records + all litigation +
> local reporting) shows the actual exempt title-holder is the *Pecos Housing Finance
> Corporation* — a Chapter 394 HFC.** The seed conflated two intertwined Pecos entities that
> share a board and an executive director. These deals are therefore **HFC (Ch. 394) deals
> that face the HB 21 §13(i) 1/1/2027 exemption cliff** — the core "going-away" target set —
> not surviving housing-authority deals.
>
> **Net scope effect:** ~126 properties move from "uncertain / likely out of scope" (Tier 3)
> into the priority Tier-1 target set, roughly *doubling* the Tier-1 count (150 → ~276).
> Confidence: **HIGH** on species = HFC / kill = HB 21; **MEDIUM** that all 126 are HFC-held
> (a minority may have pivoted to the Ch. 392 authority or a PFC — per-property CAD/deed
> verification required in Phase 3).

## 1. Legal species — HFC (Ch. 394), NOT a housing authority (Ch. 392)

**Two intertwined Pecos public corporations exist, run by the same people:**

| Entity | Statute | Role | Governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Housing Authority of the Town of Pecos City** ("Pecos Housing Authority") | **Ch. 392** | Local public housing — ~130 units in Pecos; Section 8. *Not* the statewide dealmaker. | Board (commissioners): **Cara Turn** (chair), **Maribel Alvarez** (co-chair), **Irene Dominguez** (resident commissioner), Ron Garcia, Mike Mason. Executive Director: **John Salcido** (since 2012). |
| **Pecos Housing Finance Corporation** ("Pecos HFC," PHFC; EIN 33-1666333) | **Ch. 394** (Texas Housing Finance Corporations Act, §394.001 et seq.) | **The traveling statewide multifamily tax-exemption vehicle.** The entity in every lawsuit and CAD record. | Board members: **Cara Turn, Maribel Alvarez, Irene Dominguez** — *the same three individuals as the Housing Authority board.* Executive Director tied to **John Salcido** (also the Housing Authority ED). |

The two are **sister/affiliated entities sharing governance and management**, but the multifamily
deals are papered through the **Ch. 394 HFC**, and the exemption is claimed under **§394.905**
(HFC property "exempt … as public property used for public purposes"), *not* under the housing-
authority provision (§392.005). Litigation pleadings describe Pecos HFC as "a public
instrumentality and nonprofit corporation … created and located in, and controlled by, the
officials of Pecos City, Texas," invoking "the Texas Housing Finance Corporation Act,
Tex. Loc. Gov't Code § 394.001, et seq." (Haltom City appeal).

**Affiliated PFC/HFC (the decisive Q1):** There is **no separate Ch. 303 PFC**. The affiliate
is the reverse of what the project brief anticipated — the housing authority's *sister HFC*
(Ch. 394) holds the deals. **Consequence: the HB 21 (Ch. 394) regime governs, not HB 2071
(Ch. 303).** That is *worse* for these deals than the PFC path, because HB 21 imposes a hard
2027 cliff rather than a grandfather.

> **Trap honored (per kit):** entity name + statute chapter beat the deal nickname. The market,

## 2. Exemption theory — §394.905, asserted 400+ miles from Pecos

- **Provision:** **Tex. Loc. Gov't Code §394.905** — property of an HFC is "exempt, as public
  property used for public purposes, from … all … taxes" → 100% ad valorem exemption. (NOT
  Tax Code §11.111 / §392.005, which is the housing-authority theory and does not apply here.)
- **Structure (from pleadings/reporting):** a private developer acquires an apartment complex
  in a metro (Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio …), conveys **fee title to
  Pecos HFC**, which then **leases the now-tax-exempt property back to the developer/landlord
  for ~99 years**; Pecos HFC keeps a fee — reported at **~10% of the tax savings** (compare
  ~15% cited for the related Cameron County HFC). Statutory affordability nominal: "at least
  half the units" reserved for households ≤80% AMI, with weak enforcement.
- **How proponents claimed statewide reach (pre-HB-21):** Chapter 394 HFCs asserted there was
  no *enforced* geographic limit on *ownership* (as opposed to bond issuance), so a
  Pecos-sponsored HFC could take title anywhere in Texas. Cities and the AG dispute this:
  **§394.903(a)** provides a "residential development covered by this chapter **must be located
  within the local government**." That contested clause is the retroactive hook (see §3).
- **The seed wording ("added to the ownership group" via "unrecorded sale") is consistent** with
  the fee-conveyance-plus-leaseback model and with unrecorded ground leases (the kit's recurring
  gap). It does not indicate a distinct housing-authority partnership structure.

## 3. The KILL question — multiple, overlapping kill mechanisms. These ARE going away.

Contrary to the seed's Tier-3 "separate regime, may survive" premise, the Pecos deals sit
squarely in the HFC kill zone and are under active, multi-front attack:

1. **HB 21 / SB 867 (89R), signed 5/28/2025, eff. immediately — §13(i) 2027 cliff (PRIMARY).**
   An HFC-owned development, outside the sponsor's authorized area on **9/1/2025**, is
   **ineligible for the §394.905 exemption after 1/1/2027** unless every affected host
   jurisdiction adopts an approving resolution (§394.031(d)) before then. Given cities are
   *suing* Pecos HFC (below), host resolutions are near-impossible → **exemption terminates
   1/1/2027; first full-tax bill ~Oct 2027, cash due Jan 2028.** Rep. Gary Gates (author):
   "It gives them until January 1 of 2027 … to go to the city or county where the property is
   located and get an agreement to continue."
2. **HB 21 §13(e) — early death on capital events.** Pre-5/28/2025 deals keep the exemption only
   until the earlier of the 10th tax year or the end of the first tax year after a
   refinance / title conveyance / majority beneficial-interest transfer. Many of these 126 are
   2021–22-vintage bridge-loan recaps → refi/sale triggers likely arrive *before* 2027.
3. **Injunctions already granted.** *Haltom City v. Pecos HFC*: trial court **granted injunctive
   relief 6/3/2025** barring Pecos HFC from buying or obtaining exemptions on Haltom City
   property **and enjoining the Tarrant Appraisal District from granting** them. *Arlington*
   obtained a **TRO** against Pecos HFC (Jefferson North Collins/Zenith).
4. **Appraisal-district challenge + 5-year back-assessment.** **Tarrant County** filed ARB
   petitions alleging out-of-county HFCs "illegally claimed tax-exempt status on **$974 million
   worth of property across 28 sites**," and will seek to strip the exemption "not only … for
   this year but also for any of the past **five years**" (Tax Code §11.43(i)/§25.21). TAD chief
   appraiser **Joe Don Bobbitt** is himself a defendant. This is a *retroactive* threat
   independent of the 2027 cliff.
5. **AG opinion requests (retroactive-illegality theory).** **RQ-0566-KP** (10/2/2024, re
   Pleasanton HFC/Weatherford) and **RQ-0587-KP** (3/6/2025) argue §394.903(a) *already* barred
   out-of-boundary development pre-HB-21. **No issued opinion (KP-####) found as of 7/11/2026** —
   treat as pending; but the cities are litigating the same theory directly.

**Does anything let these survive?** Only if (a) hosts adopt resolutions (implausible amid
litigation), (b) the HB 21 constitutional challenge (Texas Workforce Housing Coalition + Cameron
County, ~Sept 2025) voids the retroactive provisions — status: no injunction found; or (c) a
subset of deals turns out to run through the Ch. 392 authority/a PFC rather than the HFC
(possible for a minority; see §8). **Baseline conclusion: the 126 do NOT survive — they die on
the HFC timetable.**

  The pattern (a <13,000-person town suddenly closing dozens of metro deals) strongly implies a
  developer-services intermediary bringing deals to Pecos, but no administrator firm surfaced.
  **Open gap.** (Note: press sometimes cross-wires Cameron County HFC's ED **Mark Yates** with
  Pecos; Salcido — not Yates — is the Pecos figure.)
- **Reputational-cleanup signal:** `pecoshfc.com` has been scrubbed to present only *local*
  Pecos/Reeves County programs (home repair, down-payment assistance, homebuyer education — most
  "beginning September 2025") and states "PHFC supports residents of Pecos and Reeves County."
  The statewide multifamily tax-exemption business is absent from the site — consistent with an
  entity localizing its image post-litigation.

**VERDICT: IN the priority target set. Reclassify Tier 3 → Tier 1 (HFC, Ch. 394).**
The 126 "Pecos" deals are Pecos *Housing Finance Corporation* (Ch. 394) deals, not surviving
