# Rio Grande Valley "Traveling" HFC Cluster — Phase 1 Sponsor Profiles

**Scope:** four Rio Grande Valley small-city / county Housing Finance Corporations that hold
multifamily statewide under the Ch. 394 fee-ownership exemption — Cameron County HFC (30),
La Villa HFC (22), Edcouch Community HFC (18), Maverick County HFC (18). All four are **Tier 1
(HFC, Ch. 394)** and **HB 21 §13(i)/(e) kill-regime** targets.

**Bottom line up front:** the seed classification (HFC / Ch. 394 / Tier 1 / 100% traveling) is
**confirmed for all four** by convergent public reporting and, for Maverick and Cameron, by
primary lawsuit/formation records. Every one of the 88 seed properties across the four sits
**outside the sponsor's home county** — none holds a single in-home-county multifamily property in
the seed. The four are not run by one identifiable third-party administrator; they are self-governed
by their sponsor governments but share a defense apparatus (Texas Workforce Housing Coalition;
attorney Blake Stribling / Chasnoff | Stribling LLP) and a common ~15%-of-tax-savings business model.

_As of 2026-07-11. Confidence: `seed` → `single-source` → `cross-validated`. Primary-source species
confirmation strongest for Maverick (SOS incorporation recited in a court petition) and Cameron
(named under the "Texas Housing Finance Corporation Act" in the Williamson County suit)._

## 1. Cameron County Housing Finance Corporation ("The Cameron County Housing Finance Corporation")

**(1) Legal species.** HFC, **Local Gov't Code Ch. 394** — confirmed. The Williamson County suit
attacks it under the "Texas Housing Finance Corporation Act" (Ch. 394); its own site
(buildtoinvest.com) describes an HFC mission; all statewide coverage classifies it as an HFC.
**Distinct from "Cameron County Housing Authority"** (Ch. 392, San Benito — a separate row on our
roster with 4 properties): different statute, different sponsor mechanism, different office. Do not
merge. ⚠️ `data/sponsors.json` currently lists this HFC's `home_city` as **San Benito**, which is the
Housing *Authority's* town — likely a cross-contamination error (see §8).

**(2) Sponsor / home jurisdiction.** Sponsored by **Cameron County** (a county HFC). Home county =
Cameron. Home city ambiguous in the record: seed says *Harlingen*; CBS calls it a *Brownsville*
organization; its address of record is the Cameron County courthouse (CBS notes it is not actually
located there). Because it is a *county* HFC, the operative "home jurisdiction" for HB 21 geography is
**Cameron County**, regardless of which valley city the office sits in.

**(3) Formation / people.** Formed **1979** (Texas Standard) — a legacy HFC that pivoted to traveling
deals. Two of its Irving properties have operated since the 1980s. **Sole full-time employee /
Executive Director: Mark A. Yates.** **Board President: Sergio Gonzalez.** No third-party program
administrator identified; effectively self-run by one employee. (Cameron County Judge Eddie Treviño
Jr. is on record questioning it.) See the shared-counsel note below.

**(4) Geographic reach (traveling?).** **Yes — 100% traveling.** All 30 seed properties are
out-of-county: Dallas (5), Tarrant (4), Collin (4), Denton (3), Bexar (3), Travis (3), Nueces (3),
Harris (2), Brazoria (1), Galveston (1), Jefferson (1). **Zero in Cameron County.** CBS independently
documents Dallas, Fort Worth, McKinney, Irving, Lewisville, Euless "plus another dozen cities."

**(5) Kill regime.** HB 21 **§13(i)** — out-of-area + HFC-owned on 9/1/2025 + no host resolutions →
exemption ends **1/1/2027**. **§13(e)** — early death on refi/sale/majority transfer. The seed shows
capital events already (e.g., San Antonio 2023-03-29; Austin/San Antonio/Dallas-area Dec-2024 sales),
each of which starts a §13(e) clock. **No host resolutions found; host jurisdictions are actively
litigating** (opposite of consent).

**(6) Portfolio scale.** Seed = 30. Corroborated: CBS documents the North Texas cluster + "a dozen
other cities"; the board president said cash grew "$250,000 to $6 million" in two years on ~15% fees.
Consistent with a ~30-property statewide book.

**(7) Litigation / AG / news / regulatory.**
- **Williamson County v. Cameron County HFC** — TRO granted; targets *Siena Round Rock* and *The
  Sommery* near Round Rock; Judge Bill Gravell called it "one of the most egregious acts." (KRGV,
  ValleyCentral, KVUE, Williamson County Sun, wilcotx.gov, Texas Standard.)
- **Bexar Appraisal District** suit (Chief Appraiser Rogelio Sandoval) to restore 27 complexes /
  ~$26M — Cameron County HFC among named traveling HFCs.
- **HB 21 constitutional challenge filed in Cameron County District Court** (9/9/2025): Texas
  Workforce Housing Coalition + **Post WB Apartments, LLC** v. Bexar AD — note **Post Investment
  Group** is a partner on Cameron County HFC seed deals, tying this HFC's developer network to the
  lead challenge. Counsel: Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher and Chasnoff | Stribling.
- **CBS Texas I-Team** multi-part investigation ("Brownsville organization made millions…").
- **Freddie Mac** halted quoting traveling-HFC loans (Nov 2024); Cameron County named.

**(8) Open questions / contradictions.**
- **Home-city field wrong in `sponsors.json` (San Benito).** Fix to Cameron County / (office city TBD:
  Harlingen vs Brownsville). San Benito belongs to the *Housing Authority*.
- Confirm exact SOS filing number / 1979 charter via a primary SOS record (paid detail is out of scope).
- Per-property acquisition dates needed to sort §13(i) vs §13(e) cohorts.

## 2. La Villa Housing Finance Corporation

**(1) Legal species.** HFC, **Ch. 394** — confirmed by name and uniform reporting (grouped with
Pleasanton/Pecos/Edcouch as the "four small cities" traveling HFCs). It does **not** appear in IRS
501(c)/990 databases (ProPublica, TaxExemptWorld) — consistent with a governmental HFC instrumentality,
not an ordinary nonprofit.

**(2) Sponsor / home jurisdiction.** Sponsored by the **City of La Villa** (pop. ~1,300), **Hidalgo
County**. Home county = Hidalgo.

**(3) Formation / people.** **Formation date not confirmed via a free primary source — OPEN.** No
dedicated administrator, ED, or board roster surfaced in public reporting; a city this small has no
housing staff, so deals are almost certainly developer-driven and papered by outside counsel/structurers.
Defense handled through the coalition (Blake Stribling / Chasnoff | Stribling — see shared note).

**(4) Geographic reach (traveling?).** **Yes — 100% traveling.** All 22 seed properties out-of-county:
Dallas (12), Harris (4), Tarrant (3), Collin (1), Travis (1), Bexar (1). **Zero in Hidalgo County.**
Heavy Dallas concentration.

**(5) Kill regime.** HB 21 **§13(i)** → 1/1/2027; **§13(e)** early death on capital events. **No host
resolutions** — Fort Worth is actively seeking to enjoin it.

**(6) Portfolio scale.** Seed = 22. Reporting references La Villa among HFCs that "purchased more than
20 apartment complexes" (some phrasing conflates the Bexar-cluster total across sponsors — treat the
22 as the reliable figure; Bexar-only count is small, 1 in seed).

**(7) Litigation / news.**
- **Fort Worth** intervened (in the Arlington/Pecos TRO matter) seeking its own temporary order against
  **Pleasanton, La Villa, and Maverick County HFCs** over 13 properties / **$3.2M** (Fort Worth Report).
- Named among traveling HFCs in the **Bexar AD** 27-complex / $26M suit.
- Part of the **Texas Workforce Housing Coalition** defending the model / challenging HB 21.

**(8) Open questions.** Formation date; officers/registered agent; whether any Dallas/Tarrant deals
have refied (would trigger §13(e) individually); exact Bexar footprint.

## 3. Edcouch Community Housing Finance Corporation

**(1) Legal species.** HFC, **Ch. 394** — confirmed by name ("Community Housing Finance Corporation")
and reporting. **Distinct from the "Edcouch Housing Authority"** (Ch. 392), which is a separate
Section-8/public-housing entity in the same town — do not merge. Absent from IRS nonprofit databases
(governmental instrumentality).

**(2) Sponsor / home jurisdiction.** Sponsored by the **City of Edcouch**, **Hidalgo County**. Home
county = Hidalgo.

**(3) Formation / people.** **Formation date not confirmed via free primary source — OPEN**, but the
entity is clearly **older than Maverick's**: city agendas show legacy in-town activity (4% tax-credit
deals *Cielo Lindo*; *Los Laureles*, 23 units at 1100 E. Santa Rosa Ave., Edcouch) and governance
disputes (~2019 officer removals; a suit to recover property against a former officer, "Javier
Villalobos"). Officers are **appointed/removed by the Edcouch city government**; no third-party
administrator identified. Defense via the coalition.

**(4) Geographic reach (traveling?).** **Yes — the 18 seed properties are 100% traveling:** Harris (6),
Bexar (4), Dallas (3), Tarrant (2), Travis (2), Hays (1). **Zero in Hidalgo** among the traveling
portfolio. NOTE a nuance: unlike the pure travelers, Edcouch HFC *also* runs small in-Edcouch
tax-credit rehabs — so it is a **mixed** actor (local + traveling), but the seed's 18 are all
out-of-jurisdiction and Ch. 394-exempt. Houston/San-Antonio-weighted.

**(5) Kill regime.** HB 21 **§13(i)** → 1/1/2027 for the traveling 18; **§13(e)** on capital events.
No host resolutions found.

**(6) Portfolio scale.** Seed = 18. Corroborated by consistent grouping in statewide coverage; no
contradicting count found.

**(7) Litigation / news.** Lower *individual* litigation profile than the other three — named among the
traveling HFCs in the **Bexar AD** 27-complex / $26M suit and inside the coalition, but no
Edcouch-specific city TRO surfaced in this pass. Referenced across CBS Texas and Houston Public Media
"four small cities" coverage.

**(8) Open questions.** Formation date; current officers; the split between its in-town tax-credit
deals and the 18 traveling deals (only the latter are in scope); whether any host jurisdiction has
sued Edcouch specifically.

## 4. Maverick County Housing Finance Corporation

**(1) Legal species.** HFC, **Ch. 394** — **confirmed by primary record.** The Missouri City petition
recites that it was "incorporated as a non-profit corporation under the **Texas Housing Finance
Corporations Act** on **June 24, 2024**" with the Texas SOS.

**(2) Sponsor / home jurisdiction.** Sponsored by **Maverick County** (seat: Eagle Pass). Home county =
Maverick. Address: **500 Quarry St., Ste. 3, Eagle Pass, TX 78852**.

**(3) Formation / people.** **Formed 6/24/2024 — brand new, purpose-built for traveling deals** (18
acquisitions inside ~one year). **Board = the Maverick County Commissioners Court itself:** County
Judge **Ramsey English Cantu** and Commissioners **Roberto Ruiz, Olga M. Ramos, Rosanna "Roxi" Rios,
Gerardo "Jerry" Morales.** Self-governed by the county; no separate third-party administrator named.
Defense via the coalition.

**(4) Geographic reach (traveling?).** **Yes — 100% traveling.** All 18 seed properties out-of-county:
Tarrant (5), Harris (4), Bexar (2), Dallas (2), Collin (2), Montgomery (2), Fort Bend (1). **Zero in
Maverick County.**

**(5) Kill regime.** HB 21 **§13(i)** → 1/1/2027 for pre-5/28/2025 out-of-area holdings owned on
9/1/2025. Because formation was **6/24/2024**, part of the book likely closed *after* 5/28/2025, which
would be **barred outright** by new §394.031(c)–(d) absent host resolutions (a stronger/earlier kill
than §13(i)). **§13(e)** applies to capital events. No host resolutions — Missouri City and Fort Worth
are litigating.

**(6) Portfolio scale.** Seed = 18. CBS: the HFC "removed more than **$1.7 billion** worth of property
from North Texas tax rolls alone within the past three years" (the "three years" phrasing predates its
2024 charter and likely aggregates related coverage — treat the **$1.7B North Texas** magnitude and the
18-property seed as the reliable anchors).

**(7) Litigation / news.**
- **City of Missouri City v. Maverick County HFC + Board (official capacity)** — 268th Judicial
  District Court, Fort Bend County, filed **1/24/2025**; target **The Ranch at Sienna** (a.k.a. Ravella
  at Sienna), 8811 Sienna Springs Blvd; alleges Ch. 394 acquisition **without the city's required
  approval**; TRO sought; **project dropped** after suit. (Eagle Pass Business Journal; Houston Public
  Media.)
- **Fort Worth** temporary-order effort vs. Pleasanton/La Villa/**Maverick** (13 properties / $3.2M).
- Named in the **Bexar AD** 27-complex / $26M suit; inside the **Texas Workforce Housing Coalition**.
- **Freddie Mac** loan-quoting freeze applies to its structure.

**(8) Open questions.** Per-deal acquisition dates (which of the 18 closed before vs. after 5/28/2025 —
decisive for §13(i) vs. outright §394.031 bar); named deal structurer/bond counsel.

## Common administrator / program pattern

**No single common *third-party* program administrator is identifiable from public records.** Each of
the four is nominally **self-governed by its sponsor government**:
- **Maverick** — the County Commissioners Court sits as the HFC board (formed 6/2024).
- **Cameron** — its own board (Pres. Sergio Gonzalez) + a **single employee/ED, Mark A. Yates**.
- **Edcouch** and **La Villa** — city-appointed boards in ~1,300-person Hidalgo County towns with no
  housing staff.

What they demonstrably **share** is not one administrator but a **model and a defense apparatus**:
1. **Business model** — under-resourced RGV sponsor governments approve **out-of-jurisdiction** North
   Texas / Houston / San Antonio multifamily deals brought by developers/syndicators (e.g., **Post
   Investment Group / Post WB Apartments**, **Western Wealth Capital**, and the many partners in the
   seed), collecting **~15% of the foregone taxes** as a fee; near-zero local footprint; boards meeting
   rarely (Cameron's last posted minutes were ~6 years old at the time of CBS's report).
2. **Shared legal/coalition thread** — the **Texas Workforce Housing Coalition (TWHC)** and attorney
   **Blake Stribling / Chasnoff | Stribling LLP** (with **Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher**) defend these HFCs
   and filed the **HB 21 constitutional challenge in Cameron County District Court** (TWHC + Post WB
   Apartments v. Bexar AD, 9/9/2025). This is the closest thing to a common "administrator/counsel"
   across the cluster.
3. **Common adversaries** — Bexar Chief Appraiser **Rogelio Sandoval** (27 complexes/$26M), **Fort
   Worth**, **Arlington** (vs. Pecos), **Williamson County** (vs. Cameron), **Missouri City** (vs.
   Maverick); plus the **Freddie Mac** lender freeze (Nov 2024).

**Open (out of scope to chase):** identifying an actual common deal structurer / bond counsel /
program manager would require the recorded **ground-lease and regulatory-agreement instruments**
(county-clerk artifacts), which are excluded from this public-record-only pass. Flagged as a gap.
