# Texas Essential Housing Public Facility Corporation (TEH PFC / TEHPFC)

**Phase 1 sponsor profile — Tier 2 (PFC, Ch. 303).** Research date: 2026-07-11.
Seed portfolio: **29 properties.** Confidence: **HIGH** on species/sponsor/kill-regime;
**MED** on exact per-property grandfather status (a Phase 3 recording-date question).

> One-line verdict: TEHPFC is the archetypal **"Traveling PFC"** — a Chapter 303 public
> facility corporation created by a tiny special district (the **SH130 Municipal Management
> District No. 1**, Travis County) whose only real function is to sell its governmental
> tax-exemption statewide. Its deals are **grandfathered by HB 2071** and therefore **do NOT
> face the HFC 2027 cliff**. They die on contract terms, capital events, TDHCA audit failure,
> or appraisal-district/AG challenge — a **separate, non-cliff tier** from the HFC set.

---

## 1. Legal species — PFC (Chapter 303). CONFIRMED (primary source)

TEHPFC's own **Application for Financing** (effective Dec 1, 2021) states verbatim:
*"The Corporation is a Public Facility Corporation created under Chapter 303 of the Local
Government Code, as amended."* The exemption theory is the **§303.042(f)** ad-valorem
exemption obtained through the standard PFC **purchase/ground-leaseback** structure (fee to
the PFC → 99-year unsubordinated net ground lease back to the developer LLC → property off
the tax roll). The seed's 29 ground-lease comments ("99 year unsubordinated net ground lease
held by Texas Essential Housing Public Facility Corporation, expires 2120–2122") are the
recorded fingerprint of exactly this structure.

- Statute: **Local Government Code Ch. 303** (Public Facility Corporations Act).
- Species: **PFC** — not an HFC, not a housing authority. The name says "PFC" and the primary
  source confirms it. **Ch. 394 / HB 21 do not apply.**

## 2. Who sponsors it — SH130 Municipal Management District No. 1 (Travis County). RESOLVED

Under Ch. 303 a PFC must be created by a governmental "sponsor." TEHPFC's sponsor is **not a
city or county** — it is the **SH130 Municipal Management District No. 1 ("SH130 MMD")**, a
special district:

- **Created 2019** by special district local law — **HB 4694 (86R, 2019)** — codified at
  **Special District Local Laws Code Ch. 3971**, within the **extraterritorial jurisdiction
  of the City of Austin** along the SH 130 toll corridor. Home/most-populous county =
  **Travis** (SB 2434 fiscal note: dissolution would be "facilitated" through Travis County;
  the corridor also touches Caldwell).
- The SH130 MMD **created and sponsors TEHPFC in 2021**. Per the legislative record, the
  district "has not developed improvements or projects in the district" and "failed to reach
  an assessment agreement with the City of Austin," so its **sole purpose became serving as
  sponsor to the TEHPFC."
- **Boundaries that define "traveling":** the sponsor's authorized area is the narrow SH130
  MMD strip in Austin's ETJ. **None of the 29 properties sit inside it.** They are in Austin,
  Round Rock, San Antonio, Houston, Seabrook, Dallas, Arlington, Saginaw, College Station and
  Midland — i.e., **every deal is out-of-boundary. This is a statewide-operating traveling
  PFC**, and TEHPFC is the deal the 2023 reform literature literally calls "the Traveling PFC."

**Home-county fix for the seed:** sponsor home county = **Travis** (Austin). Traveling flag for
the 29 = **effectively all** (statewide). This resolves the seed's "home county UNKNOWN."

- **Fee schedule (primary source — the Dec 2021 application):** annual **10% of property-tax
  savings** (new construction) / **15%** (acquisition-rehab) + **25% of sales-tax savings**;
  one-time **$500,000 developer fee** (ground-up); **$100/unit annual administrative fee**;
  **10% capital-event participation** on refi/sale proceeds above a return hurdle; $3,000
  application fee; ground-lease-structure legal fees capped at $300,000. Set-aside required:
  **≥50% of units at ≤80% AMI** (the pre-HB 2071 §303.042(f) standard; acq-rehab adds 10% at
  ≤60% and 2% at ≤30% AMI). Using the **old 50%/80% covenant confirms these are legacy
  §303.042(f)-era deals**, not the HB 2071 10%+40% tiered regime.

## 4. Grandfather analysis — mostly pre-HB 2071; a boundary subset is fact-dependent

**Controlling law:** HB 2071 (88R) added **§303.021(d)** requiring PFC multifamily to sit
inside the sponsor's boundaries, **effective June 18, 2023.** Its transition rule
(**HB 2071 §10**, verified against enrolled text) grandfathers a development that was
**"approved by a public facility corporation or the sponsor … before the effective date"** (or
acquired before it) — such deals stay under **prior law** (no in-boundary requirement).
**Grandfather turns on the approval/acquisition date, not the ground-lease-commencement date.**

**Vintage distribution (from the 29 ground-lease expiration dates; 99-yr leases):**
- Ground leases commence **~Dec 2021 → late 2023** (expirations 2120–2122). Program launched
  Dec 2021.
- **Clearly grandfathered:** the 2021 → early/mid-2023 cohort (leases expiring 2120–2121 and
  early-2122) — approved/closed before June 18, 2023.
- **Boundary subset (needs per-property recording dates):** roughly a dozen have ground leases
  **commencing after June 18, 2023** (expirations mid/late-2122 — e.g., 06/20, 06/30, 10/04,
  10/11, 10/25, 10/27, 11/01/2122). Whether these are grandfathered depends on **when the
  SH130 MMD/TEHPFC board *approved* them** (official-intent resolutions are valid 12 months,
  so an Oct-2023 closing can trace to an early-2023 approval). If a given deal was **not**
  approved before June 18, 2023, it violates §303.021(d) (out-of-boundary) and its exemption
  is **directly attackable** — this is precisely the fight now in the Williamson CAD suits.

**Net:** most of the 29 are grandfathered pre-2023-regime PFC deals; a minority sit on the
June-18-2023 boundary and carry live grandfather risk. Exact split is a **Phase 3
recording-date task** (pull SWD/ground-lease and board-approval dates per property).

## 5. Kill regime — NO 2027 cliff; dies on contract/capital event/audit/challenge

**There is no HB 21-style 2027 guillotine here.** HB 21 (89R, 2025) amends **Ch. 394 (HFCs)
only**; its §13(i) 1/1/2027 termination is an HFC provision and **does not reach Ch. 303
PFCs** — confirmed. So **these 29 are not in the "going away in 2027" set.** What actually ends
a grandfathered PFC exemption:

1. **Contract / lease term.** Exemption rides the PFC's fee ownership and the ground-lease /
   bond period. (HB 2071 capped *new* PFC exemptions at 60 yrs new / 30 yrs stabilized —
   grandfathered deals keep prior, longer terms; the 99-yr leases run to 2120–2122.)
2. **Capital event — sale, refinance, or PFC ceasing to own.** If the PFC conveys the fee (or
   the structure unwinds), the property returns to the roll. The application's own 10%
   capital-event fee shows sales/refis are contemplated triggers.
3. **§303.0426 TDHCA audit / compliance regime.** HB 2071 + TDHCA rules require annual PFC
   audit reports (first due Dec 1, 2024, then June 1 annually). Non-compliance/non-filing is
   the audit-vulnerability the kit flags. ⚠️ **TEHPFC was not visible on the TDHCA PFC
   compliance-monitoring list during this pass** — either a data-lag/list-incompleteness issue
   or a **non-filer distress signal**; flag for verification (TPIA TDHCA).
   *(Note: the granular "$40/unit retroactive audit fee" claim remains REFUTED — §303.0426
   only requires the audit "at the expense of the public facility user," no per-unit fee.)*
4. **Appraisal-district challenge / litigation.** CADs are denying/attacking the out-of-area
   exemptions and TEHPFC is litigating (see §7). An adverse judgment — or a future AG opinion
   agreeing the out-of-boundary model fails the "direct benefit" test (KP-0437 was skeptical
   but declined to rule) — could unwind exemptions, with **Tax Code §11.43(i) back-assessment
   reaching 5 preceding years.**
5. **Legislative kill of the sponsor.** **SB 2434 (88R, 2023)** would have **dissolved the
   SH130 MMD** by 1/1/2024 and routed residual assets to a taxing-unit compensation trust —
   it **passed the Senate but died in House Urban Affairs.** The threat can return (89R/90R);
   dissolving the sponsor would put the PFC's continued existence and its exemptions in doubt.

**No knowable single "kill date."** Estimate: no statutory cliff; unwinds occur
**deal-by-deal** on sale/refi, on any successful CAD/AG challenge, or on audit forfeiture —
plus a live but contingent legislative-dissolution risk.

## 6. Portfolio scale — 29 corroborated (~30 statewide)

Seed = **29**. The Williamson County Sun reported **"at least 30 apartment complexes across
Texas"** using this exemption mechanism; TPPF/press describe deals "all over the state"
(San Antonio, Houston, etc.). Counties represented in the seed: **Travis, Williamson, Bexar,
Harris, Tarrant, Dallas, Brazos, Midland.** The independent ~30 figure and the multi-county
spread **corroborate the seed's 29** and the statewide/traveling character. (Ignore the
Sun's "since 2016" phrasing — that describes §303.042(f) PFC deals generally; TEHPFC itself
dates from 2021.)

## 7. Litigation / AG / regulatory / news exposure — HIGH

- **TEHPFC v. Williamson Central Appraisal District**, 395th Dist. Ct., Williamson County —
  **Cause 24-0022-C395**; and **TEHPFC (Enclave La Frontera) v. Williamson CAD — Cause
  24-1698-C395.** PFC-side suits contesting the CAD's treatment of the out-of-area exemption;
  the live test of the traveling-PFC exemption. (Status not resolved this pass.)
- **AG Opinion KP-0437 (Mar 8, 2023)**, on Sen. Bettencourt's request: whether a PFC sponsored
  by a **municipal management district** may act **outside the district's territory.** AG
  **declined a definitive answer** (fact/policy question), but its **footnote questioned**
  whether housing "in counties well outside the boundaries of the district directly benefits
  property in the district" (§375.092(f)) — a standing skepticism that a court could adopt.
- **SB 2434 (88R, 2023)** — Bettencourt's bill to **dissolve the SH130 MMD**; passed Senate
  5/8/2023, **died** in House Urban Affairs. Names the district/PFC and the tax-shift harm.
- **Williamson County Commissioners investigation** into TEHPFC exemptions in the county
  (Commissioners Covey/Long/Boles); Covey: *"it's a scam to the taxpayers."*
- **TPPF exposé** ("How an Austin Special Purpose District Lined Its Pockets") — alleges
  conflicts with board-member-tied businesses; cites a Houston Chronicle investigation.
- Named repeatedly in the 2023 reform record as the paradigm "Traveling PFC" that motivated
  HB 2071.
