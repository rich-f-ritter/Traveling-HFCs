## 1. Challenge status — does anything move the 2027 cliff?

**Bottom line: NO. No injunction, TRO, or ruling has stayed HB 21 or moved the 1/1/2027 cliff. It is
still fully statutory and in force.** The coalition suit *seeks* a declaration that HB 21 is
unconstitutional (which, if ultimately granted, could vacate or move the cliff), but as of the latest
public coverage the case is at the pleading/intervention stage — no merits ruling and no injunctive
relief against the statute.

### The case

- **Style:** *Texas Workforce Housing Coalition (TWHC) and Post WB Apartments, LLC v. Bexar Appraisal
  District.*
- **Filed:** September 9, 2025, **Cameron County District Court** (home venue of the Cameron County HFC
  entities behind the co-plaintiff property).
- **Case No.:** reported as **2025-DCL-04994** (per National Law Review coverage; Cameron Co. district-clerk
  numbering — treat as as-reported until portal-confirmed).
- **Counsel:** **Trey Cox, Gibson Dunn & Crutcher** (lead). (Consistent with the Phase-1 note that the RGV
  HFCs / Pleasanton / Pecos defend jointly through TWHC / Gibson Dunn.)
- **Claims:** HB 21 (passed May 2025) unconstitutionally (a) impairs the obligation of contracts and
  (b) is impermissibly retroactive under the Texas Constitution (Art. I, §16), by revoking Chapter 394
  tax exemptions developers relied on. Plaintiffs ask the court to declare HB 21 unconstitutional and to
  affirm that "Old Chapter 394" permitted HFCs to own property **outside** their home jurisdiction.
- **Trigger:** although HB 21 does not fully bite existing deals until **1/1/2027**, appraisal districts
  (Bexar here; Dallas AD referenced in later TWHC materials) have **already begun reversing** previously
  granted exemptions — the alleged "weaponization" that gave plaintiffs standing now.

### Posture / what to watch

- **Intervention (pro-HB21 side):** **APTBP, LLC** — a Baytown apartment operator whose registered
  agent/owner is **Rep. Gary Gates (R-Richmond)**, an HB 21 co-sponsor — filed a *petition in
  intervention* through the group **"Concerned Property Tax Payers of Texas,"** arguing it pays full
  property tax while HFC deals are 100% exempt. Hearing on the intervention was **not yet scheduled** at
  time of reporting. (Notable irony: Gates also co-signed AG request RQ-0566-KP that seeded the crackdown.)
- **Related front:** TWHC also filed a **petition to intervene in Williamson County's suit against the
  Cameron County HFC**, which seeks a judgment that CCHFC lacked authority to acquire or exempt property
  outside Cameron County. (This is the Phase-1 "Williamson County v. Cameron County HFC" matter.)
- **Distinguish the property-level injunctions** already in our roster (Haltom City v. Pecos HFC 6/3/2025;
  Pleasanton TIs in Lake Worth/Arlington/Fort Worth/Missouri City; Hays CAD TROs; Maverick/Missouri City
  TRO). Those are city-vs-HFC or CAD-vs-HFC fights over *individual* exemptions — **none stays HB 21 or the
  2027 date.** The only case that could move the cliff globally is this constitutional challenge, and it
  has produced no such order yet.

**Implication for the census:** the 2027 cliff assumption for the 276 Tier-1 properties **holds**. Flag
the TWHC constitutional challenge and the APTBP intervention as the single event that could shift it;
re-check the Cameron Co. docket for any temporary-injunction or plea-to-the-jurisdiction ruling.

## 2. AG opinion requests RQ-0566-KP and RQ-0587-KP

Both requests asked the AG whether a Chapter 394 HFC may own/develop residential property **outside** the
boundaries of the local government that formed it, and if so whether such property qualifies for the 100%
exemption. **Both are now CLOSED with NO opinion issued** — the questions were overtaken/mooted by the
Legislature enacting HB 21 (eff. 5/28/2025), which answered them by statute. HB 21 is effectively the
"successor" to both requests.

### RQ-0566-KP — the one that names the template

- **Requestors:** **Rep. J.M. Lozano (Chair)** and **Rep. Gary Gates (Vice Chair), House Committee on
  Urban Affairs.** Dated **October 2, 2024.** Status: **Closed — no associated opinion.**
- **Named template deal (the load-bearing find):** a proposed **Memorandum of Understanding between
  Pleasanton Housing Finance Corporation and developer "Elevate CIG"** for an **existing multifamily
  development in Weatherford, Texas (Parker County).** Structure described: Elevate CIG (record owner)
  conveys the property to the HFC; HFC grants a **99-year ground lease** to a limited partnership owned
  by the HFC, the developer, and an investor; developer pays all costs; HFC takes only nominal fees /
  cash-flow; parties claim **100% property-tax and construction-materials sales-tax exemption**; MOU lets
  the private party recover fee title for nominal consideration if the exemption is lost in the first 20
  years. (Pleasanton = Atascosa Co.; property = Parker Co., ~450 mi away.)
- **Legal theory (foreshadows HB 21):** unlike Ch. 303 (PFCs), **§394.903(a)** states "a residential
  development covered by this chapter must be located within the local government," so the committee argued
  Ch. 394 does **not** allow out-of-jurisdiction HFC ownership — the exact position HB 21 codified.
- Source (full text extracted):
  https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/request-files/request/2024/RQ0566KP.pdf

### RQ-0587-KP — the successor request

- **Requestor:** **House Committee on Intergovernmental Affairs.** Dated **March 6, 2025.** Status:
  **Closed — no associated opinion.**
- Same subject line (Ch. 394 applied to an HFC's residential development outside the local-government
  boundary) — a near-duplicate of RQ-0566-KP routed through the successor committee. The request file is a
  **scanned image (no extractable text layer)**, so specific named deals in it could not be harvested from
  the free PDF; the AG index page confirms the subject, date, requestor, and closed/no-opinion status.
- Source: https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/requests/ken-paxton/rq-0587-kp
  (file: .../request-files/request/2025/RQ0587KP.pdf)

## 3. Texas Workforce Housing Coalition — membership & repeat private partners

TWHC does **not publish a member roster** (its site lists only categories: "housing providers, attorneys,
policy advocates, politicians, consultants, local housing authorities & corporations"; membership by
emailing Info@TexasWorkforceHousingCoalition.com). Membership therefore has to be inferred from filings.
What the reform-fight documents surface:

- **Sponsor members (already in our roster):** Cameron County HFC (Willowbend / Post WB; Williamson
  intervention) and — via the shared Gibson Dunn/TWHC defense described in Phase 1 — the RGV HFCs,
  Pleasanton HFC, and Pecos HFC.
- **Repeat private partners / developers now identified (NEW to our tracking):**
  - **Elevate CIG** — Pleasanton HFC's counterparty on the Weatherford deal (RQ-0566-KP).
  - **Post Investment Group** (LA; principal **Jason Post**) — Cameron County HFC counterparty on
    Willowbend (San Antonio); co-plaintiff via **Post WB Apartments, LLC**.
  - **Waterford Property Company** (co-founder **John Drachman**) — developer quoted in TWHC challenge
    coverage; active in Texas HFC/PFC sale-leaseback workforce-housing deals.
- **Adjacent lead (separate fight, NEW sponsor not in roster):** **Corpus Christi Housing Authority**'s
  **"Workforce Housing Opportunity (W.H.O.)" program** (~13 apartment complexes, Nueces Co.), defended at
  saveworkforcehousing.com against a Nueces County Commissioners-funded Open Meetings Act suit. A Ch. 392
  authority (no statutory 2027 cliff) but a workforce-housing sponsor worth a Phase-3 property sweep.
